# ADR-212: A Sequence Says What It Is as a Whole — a Range Replays, a Pipeline Walks From Its Back End, and Every Taking Is Seen

**Status:** Superseded by [ADR-293](../../specification/adr/adr-293.md) — its D1-D5 are D16-D20 there
**Date:** September 25, 2026
**Target Version:** Nikaia 0.0.188
**Answers:** feedback the owner brought in from outside and then decided: *the
repairs first — a range in a name and a sequence walked twice reach `rustc`,
which breaks Part III C.1* — and *reversing a pipeline must not force a
`collect()` into a list*. The owner's decision: build the repairs, a range's
`rev` and `step_by`, and the words a pipeline needs, in one step; **leave the
two-ended cursor out on purpose** (§4)
**Refines:** [ADR-105](../../specification/adr/adr-105.md) D1 (`Seq[T]`'s words) and D2 (the once-only
rule, `NK2702`); [ADR-137](adr-137.md) D4 (*a range is an ordinary expression:
it may be given a name*, which it could not be walked under)
**Related:** [ADR-172](../../specification/adr/adr-172.md) D1 (the step's three states, beside which
these three stand), [ADR-094](../../specification/adr/adr-094.md) D4 (a `for` lends), [ADR-048](adr-048.md)
D1 (no `usize` a program meets), [ADR-054](adr-054.md) D2 (a count converted
where it is handed over)

## 1. Context

Four programs, each a pre-alpha tester's first hour, and where each ended:

| | program | before |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| a | `let r = 0..<3` then `for i in r { … }` | `rustc`: *no method named `iter` found for `Range`* |
| b | `let ks = m.keys()` then `for k in ks { … }` | `rustc`: the same, for `Keys` |
| c | `let s = xs.iter().map(…)`, then `let t = s`, then `s.count()` | `rustc`: *use of moved value* |
| d | the latest three moving averages of a list | `windows`, `rev`, `take` did not exist; `map` handed back `Seq[?]` |

(a) is the sharpest: Part I 6.x has said since [ADR-137](adr-137.md) that a
range *may be given a name*, and the checker had no type for one at all.
(c) is `NK2702`'s own subject — a sequence is walked once — seen only where a
`for` or a method walked it, never where a `let`, an argument, a loop or a
lambda took it.

The feedback that raised (d) asked for more than a `rev` on containers, and it
was right in two of its three arguments. A range is not a container, and
`(0..<n).rev()` should not lay one out. And a pipeline — `xs.iter().map(f)` —
is a sequence, so a `rev` that only containers had would make every reversal
of a pipeline a `collect()` first: the heap copy the language exists to avoid.
The third argument, a stream consumed from both ends at once, is §4.

## 2. What the language below knows that this language did not

Rust answers *can this be walked from the back?* with a trait,
`DoubleEndedIterator`, and *is its length known?* with another,
`ExactSizeIterator`, and several adapters need **both**: `take`, `zip`, `skip`
and `step_by` walked from the back have to know where the back is. A `map` or a
`filter` needs only the first. So a single word — "reversible" — is wrong in
one direction or the other: it either refuses `chars().rev()` (no length) or
allows `lines.filter(f).zip(ys).rev()` (no length where one is needed), and
either way `rustc` answers about a file nobody wrote.

And one fact the language below gets wrong for this language's integer: Rust's
`Range<i64>` has **no** `ExactSizeIterator` — its length may not fit a
`usize` on a 32-bit target — so `(0..n).step_by(2).rev()` does not compile
there for `n: i64`, which is every `n` a program has.

## 3. Decisions

### D1 — Three words after `Seq[T]` say what the sequence is as a whole.

Beside the step's `sync`, `pauses` and `throws`:

* **`ends`** — it can be walked from the back. A range, a list's `iter()`,
  `drain()`, `chars()`, `windows`, `chunks`.
* **`sized`** — its length is known before it is walked. The same, and a map's
  `keys()`, `values()` and `drain()`; not `chars()`, and not a `filter`.
* **`replays`** — walking it does not use it up. Only a range (D3).

Like every word on a `Seq`, none is in a program's type grammar
([ADR-105](../../specification/adr/adr-105.md) D4): the ledger names them and the checker reads them,
and a message never says them (D2).

### D2 — On a receiver they are a demand; on a result they pass through.

`Seq::rev` writes its receiver `Seq[$T] ends`. A sequence that lacks the word is
refused with **`NK2703`** — *`rev` walks a sequence from the back, and this one
can only be walked from the front* — in the program's words, with the way out
(`collect()` it, and walk the list from the back). `io::lines().rev()` is the
case it was written for.

A result's words **pass through**: the result has a word where the entry writes
it **and every sequence handed in has it too**. So `map` writes `ends sized` and
a `map` over `io::lines()` has neither, while a `map` over a list's `iter()` has
both. One ledger column, `ends_by_length = true`, says the result can be walked
from the back only where every input's **length** is known — `take`, `zip`,
`skip`, `step_by` — which is §2's second trait, said once per entry rather than
guessed per adapter.

### D3 — A range is a sequence that replays, and below it is a value.

`a..<b` and `a..b` are `Seq[T] sync ends sized replays`, with `T` whichever end
has a type. `NK2702` never applies to one: walking a range walks a copy of two
numbers.

Below, a range that is **kept, handed on or called on** is
`nikaia_std::range::Span` (`..<`) or `Through` (`..`): `Copy`, its own iterator,
with a length for every integer type, so every walk takes a copy and `rustc`
agrees that `r` is still there. It slices through `index::at` as a written
range does. A range written **straight into a `for` or into brackets** stays
Rust's `a..b`: it is walked once, or it is a slice, and needs nothing more.

### D4 — Every place a sequence is taken is seen, and a named one is walked by value.

Every read of a name that holds a sequence **takes** it: a `let` that names it
again, an argument, a `return`, a field, and every method, since a sequence has
none that looks without walking. Two reads do not take: an assignment's target
(`s = xs.iter()` gives it a new one) and a `&`. `NK2702` then refuses the
second use, as it did for a `for`.

**And the one the statement order cannot see**: a sequence declared outside a
loop and taken inside it is gone on the next turn; one taken inside a lambda is
gone on the next call, since what the lambda is handed to may run it more than
once. Both are `NK2702` where they are taken — unless the loop gives the name a
new sequence in its body or may leave (`break`, `return`) on the turn that took
it, which is over-approximated in the direction that refuses less.

**Two arms of one choice are not one after the other.** Ordered by statement
alone, a sequence taken in the `then` of an `if` and read in its `else` was
refused — a correct program — and once a list's `iter()` is a sequence that
would have been every such program. Each take and each read now carries which
arm of which `if` or `match` it stands in, and a read in another arm of the
same choice is not after the take; a read after the whole choice is.

A `for` over a **name** that holds a sequence is handed the sequence rather
than lent it: no `.iter()` below, and the binding is each element itself.

### D5 — The pipeline, none of it allocating.

`map` keeps its element type: `fn($T) -> $U` binds `$U` from what the lambda's
body comes to — a fact about a block the checker has walked, as `spawn`'s is,
and not a type written for the lambda ([ADR-029](adr-029.md) D1 is about
*writing* one). Where the parameter's type states a result, that stands.

New entries: `Seq::rev`, `Seq::zip`, `Seq::take`, `Seq::skip`, `Seq::step_by`,
and on a list `Vec::iter` (where a pipeline over a list starts, lent),
`Vec::windows` and `Vec::chunks` (runs that are views, not copies). Each is the
language below's own adapter, so a chain of them is one loop. A count — `take`,
`skip`, `step_by`, `nth`, `chunks`, `windows` — is an `i64` here and a `usize`
below; the checker names those arguments **by the entry the call resolved to**
and the emitter converts them, so a program's own method called `take` keeps
its `i64`.

`enumerate` is not among them: the position it hands out is a `usize`, which
is not a type a program meets ([ADR-048](adr-048.md) D1), and converting inside
a tuple is a shape of its own.

## 4. What is left out on purpose

**A stream consumed from both ends at once** — a cursor with a `next()` and a
`next_back()`, narrowed from both sides. A `Seq` has no `next()` at all; it is
walked by `for` and the consumers, and a cursor is a new abstraction rather
than a word on this one. Narrowing text or a list from both sides is index
arithmetic on views and works today without allocating (`ref t[i..<j]` is
`&t[i..j]` below); a parser that narrows a *stream* goes through a grammar. The
owner decided to leave it out until a program needs it.

## 5. Found on the way, and not decided here

*All answered since: the first bullet by [ADR-213](adr-213.md), the last by
[ADR-214](adr-214.md), the rest by [ADR-215](adr-215.md).*

* **Writing into a map keyed by an owned `String`** reaches `rustc`:
  `m["a"] = 1` and `m[key] = 1` on a `HashMap[String, _]` (`index::at` has no
  arm for a `String`, and a literal is not converted there). Every example keys
  by `ref String`, which works. *Answered by [ADR-213](adr-213.md), 0.0.189.*
* `HashMap::insert` has no entry, so `m.insert(k, v)` is not checked; the
  language's form is `m[k] = v`.
* `str::chars` says `-> ?`, where `String::chars` says `Seq[char] sync ends`.
* A slice view has no type in the checker, so `chunks` and `windows` hand out
  `ref Vec[$T]`, whose `len` and `iter` agree with the slice below.
* A range kept in a name whose ends are **both literals**, used to slice a
  **list**, leaves `rustc` an integer type to guess; it did before this record.
* Two takes of one sequence **within one statement** (`s.count() + s.count()`)
  are not told apart, because `NK2702` orders reads by statement, as `NK2101`
  does.

## 6. What it costs

A kept range is two integers and a copy per walk; the copy is what the language
below's own `Range` does on every `clone()`, and the stepping inlines to the
same loop. A range in a `for` is unchanged. The checker reads three more words
on a type it already compared, and walks one more stack of frames on a read of
a sequence name.
