# ADR-178: Tier-1 Staging Is Withdrawn, and What Would Reopen It Is a Measured Crossover

**Status:** Accepted · **Built:** the absence is the decision — see §5
**Date:** September 21, 2026
**Target Version:** Nikaia 0.0.126
**Answers:** [`open-decisions.md`](../../open-decisions.md)'s *is tier-1 staging
withdrawn, or is it still on the list?*
**Related:** [ADR-310](adr-310.md) (which named the two tiers and called Tier 1
the one that waits on nothing), [`docs/history/staging-candidates.md`](../../history/staging-candidates.md)
(the survey, and the rule it ends on), [ADR-276](adr-276.md) D1 (the precedent for
the shape: *the absence is a decision rather than an omission*),
[ADR-010](adr-010.md) (the staging this language actually shipped, and what made
it right), [ADR-276](adr-276.md) (a cost priced before the thing was built)

---

## 1. Context

[ADR-310](adr-310.md) split staging in two. **Tier 2** is a user-facing
metaprogramming surface and waits on questions nobody has answered. **Tier 1** is
the compiler doing the staging itself — recognising a shape in the source and
emitting a better one — and that record said it *waits on nothing and is where
the interesting applications live*. It went onto the roadmap as a `[ ]`, in the
language's count, and stayed there.

[`docs/history/staging-candidates.md`](../../history/staging-candidates.md) is the survey that
followed. It named every candidate anybody could find, wrote down the ones that
look like candidates and are not, built a measurement harness, and measured.
**Every candidate it named is now closed**, and three of them closed against the
survey's own prediction:

* **A route table's perfect hash** — [ADR-310](adr-310.md)'s own headline
  example — has no target. There is no HTTP server, and building one is a
  feature rather than a first step.
* **`match` over string literals** is already what `rustc` does, and no `.nika`
  file in the tree writes one over text.
* **Character classes and terminator sets** are solved upstream and measured:
  three inclusive ranges eight bytes at a time, and a literal set collected at
  macro time. Every Nikaia grammar uses at most two needles. No headroom.
* **A template's `String::with_capacity`** is worth 3.4 % on a static page and
  **0.0 %** on a growing one.
* **`html::escape`** is 65 % cheaper — and the *table* the survey recommended was
  **16 % worse** than what it replaced, because 4 KB of fat pointers is not a
  register.
* **First-byte dispatch for literal alternations**, which the survey called *the
  actual prize*, is **under 3 %** of the parse. The profile taken before any code
  was written put the whitespace those alternations were re-skipping at **56 %**,
  and hoisting it is **−19.8 %** — merged, in a dependency, and not staging at
  all.

So the box on the roadmap is not unbuilt work. It is a direction this project
examined, in writing, with a harness, and did not take.

## 2. Decisions

### D1 — Tier-1 staging is **withdrawn**. The absence is a decision and not an omission.

The box leaves `project_status_and_roadmap.md` the way `loop` left Part I
([ADR-276](adr-276.md) D1): the language area is counted **out of 25 rather than
26**, and what a reader wondering where it went finds is this record.

**A `[ ]` on a progress page is a promise.** It says somebody intends to build
this, and every other `[ ]` on the page borrows credibility from the ones that
are honest. Keeping a box nobody intends to open makes the whole column worth
less, which is the cost that decided this and not the cost of the record.

### D2 — What would reopen it is **a candidate with a measured crossover**, measured before the complexity is paid.

This is [`staging-candidates.md`](../../history/staging-candidates.md)'s own closing rule,
raised here from a notes page to a record: *a staging decision enters the compiler
only together with a measured crossover; without one the complexity is certain
and the gain is not, and the complexity is paid twice — once in the language
surface and once in the generated code.*

**So the door is a measurement and not a mood.** Anybody may reopen this, and
what they bring is a profile of a real workload showing where the time is, and a
threshold read off the numbers rather than off the shape of the problem. The
survey has two of its own counter-examples for the second half: `until(a | b)`
switches at three needles, and the crossover in
[`upstream/winnow-grammar-findings.md`](../../upstream/winnow-grammar-findings.md)
§5 sat at three characters where six to eight had been assumed.

### D3 — [ADR-310](adr-310.md) is not reversed, and neither is Tier 2's position.

That record's *Tier 1 waits on nothing* was true and is still true: nothing blocks
it. What this record adds is that waiting on nothing is not the same as being
worth doing, and four years of candidates produced none that measured.

**Tier 2 keeps its own status**, which is unanswered questions rather than closed
candidates. The two were always separate, and the survey's last line stays the
useful one: *Tier 1 does not need Tier 2, and the applications people cite as the
reason for Tier 2 are all in this list.*

### D4 — The harness stays.

`crates/nikaia/tests/measure.rs` and the workloads in `benches/` were built for
this item and are the reason it could be closed rather than argued about. They
are not tied to staging: what they measure is this compiler and the programs it
emits, and the next question about either is cheaper for their existing.

## 3. Consequences

* **The roadmap's language area is 24.5 of 25**, and the overall count is 28.5 of
  39. Nothing was built to move those numbers; a promise nobody intended to keep
  stopped being counted as one.
* **The survey stays on the shelf**, cited by this record, because what it
  contains is mostly *findings* — measurements that are true whatever is decided
  about staging, and three of which overturned an intuition.
* **One question leaves [`open-decisions.md`](../../open-decisions.md)**, which is
  what an answer does.
* **What it costs if this is wrong**: a record to undo, which is the price
  [ADR-276](adr-276.md) paid for `loop` and is small — and there is not even a
  word to un-reserve here.

## 4. What this does not decide

* **Tier 2** ([ADR-310](adr-310.md)), which is a different question with
  different blockers and keeps its own status.
* **Whether the compiler may stage at all.** It already does, and
  [ADR-010](adr-010.md) is the shipped example: the hasher a map gets is chosen
  from where its keys came from, measured at 26 % on the flagship, with nothing
  in the source mentioning hashing. That is what a Tier-1 item looks like when it
  is right — decided by an analysis the compiler already runs, and measured
  before it was built. [ADR-311](adr-311.md)'s fixed map is the second, and its
  shape was **changed** by the measurement rather than confirmed by it.
* **The ≤3 % ceiling in a dependency**, which is a real number and a bad place to
  spend the next measurement.

## 5. What is built

**Nothing, which is the decision.** The box is gone from
`project_status_and_roadmap.md` and the language area is counted out of 25; the
percentages in the table at the top are recounted from the boxes by
`scripts/check-roadmap-numbers.py`, so a withdrawal that forgot the number would
fail the build rather than sit there.

[`docs/history/staging-candidates.md`](../../history/staging-candidates.md) keeps its findings and
gains a status line saying the item they belong to is closed — the same thing
this record says, said where somebody reading the survey will be standing.
