# ADR-234: A Lazy Walk of `io::lines()` Carries Its Failure to the Walk

**Status:** Accepted · **Built:** yes, in 0.0.220
**Date:** September 27, 2026
**Target Version:** Nikaia 0.0.220
**Answers:** `open-work.md` §2.2, *a lazy walk of `io::lines()` has no shape*
**Refines:** [ADR-172](adr-172.md) D3 and D5 (the lazy walks of a pausing
sequence, refused from the lowering), [ADR-233](adr-233.md) §3
**Related:** [ADR-025](adr-025.md) D1 (a walk of a failing sequence fails at
the walk), Part I 6.4 (a failure is never a shorter list)

## 1. Context

[ADR-233](adr-233.md) gave a sequence whose step pauses its lazy walks, on
`nikaia_std::seq::Step`, and left the one sequence in `std` whose step also
**fails**:

```nika
let long = io::lines().map(fn(l) { l.len() }).filter(fn(n) { n > 80 }).collect()
```

It was refused by the lowering, *write the loop*. Below, a step of
`io::lines()` is the line or the failure, so an adapter over it has to decide
where the failure goes.

## 2. Decisions

### D1 — The lambda sees the item, and the failure goes on to the walk.

`io::Lines` is a `Step` whose item is the line or the failure, and every lazy
walk `Seq` has but `rev` — `map`, `filter`, `take`, `skip`, `step_by`, `zip`,
with a plain lambda or one that pauses — hands back another sequence of the
same kind, `nikaia_std::seq::Failing`. A lambda is handed the **line**; a
failure passes through it untouched. What walks the result is where the
failure lands, which is where [ADR-025](adr-025.md) D1 already put it:

* `collect`, `count`, `nth` and `join` end at the first failure and hand it
  back, and the `?` after them is the one the eager walk of `io::lines()` has
  had since ADR-172 D5;
* a `for` over it takes each step's `?`, so the lines before the failure are
  handled and the failure leaves the function after them.

So `map` of a failing sequence is a failing sequence: the type of the result
keeps the receiver's `throws`, and a function walking it says `throws` as for
the lines themselves (`NK2701`). **A failure is never a shorter list**
(Part I 6.4): `take`, `skip` and `step_by` count a failure as a step, and the
walk ends at it all the same.

### D2 — The net stays.

With D1, no entry that hands back a sequence lacks a pausing form, and the
lowering's refusal for one (ADR-172 D5) is not reached. It stays, worded for
the next entry that is added without one, rather than being taken out and put
back.

## 3. Consequences

`open-work.md` §2.2 is closed and deleted. The README's wall *do I/O inside a
lambda handed to `std`* is gone. Programs that compiled before compile to the
same Rust.

## 4. What is built

All of it, in 0.0.220: `Failing` and its adapters in
`crates/nikaia-std/src/seq.rs`, `io::Lines` as a `Step` with the lazy walks,
the checker's chain over any pausing sequence with the receiver's `throws`
carried, and `crates/nikaia/tests/pausing_steps.rs`'s run of a lazy walk and a
`for` over one with a broken line on standard input.
