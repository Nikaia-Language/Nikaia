# ADR-189: `?.` Lends Its Receiver Where That Needs No Representation, And The Third Case Waits On The Tether

**Status:** Accepted · **Built:** two of three — see §5
**Date:** September 22, 2026
**Target Version:** Nikaia 0.0.140
**Builds on:** [ADR-113](adr-113.md), whose D1 and D3 this implements for every
case that does not need a state the compiler does not have
**Related:** [ADR-052](adr-052.md) D8 (the translated note, now narrowed),
[ADR-066](adr-066.md) D3 (the `match` a reached method lowers to),
[ADR-008](adr-008.md) (the tether), [ADR-094](adr-094.md) D4 (a `let` over a
place is a view of it — the same sentence one construct away)

## 1. Context

[ADR-113](adr-113.md) D1 says `?.` takes nothing, and
[Part I 3.5](../10-nikaia-light.md) already writes it. The compiler did not do
it, and what a program heard instead was the language below's:

```text
error[E0382]: use of moved value: `user`
   |  help: consider calling `.as_ref()` or `.as_mut()` to borrow the type's contents
   |  help: you can `clone` the value and consume it, but this might not be your
   |        desired behavior
```

Two hints naming things this language does not have — [ADR-008](adr-008.md) D5
says a copy is written by the program and never inserted — about a file nobody
wrote ([Part III C.1](../30-nikaia-tooling.md)).

**Reading the record against the code found that it is not one piece of work.**
D2 gives the reach two answers: a member that **copies** comes out copied, and
a member that does not comes out as a **view of the receiver**. The second is
the state Part I 6.6 calls Tethered, and it is not built —
[#196](https://github.com/Nikaia-Language/Nikaia/issues/196) is *at least four change packages*,
of which the representation is the expensive half. So one third of this record
sits behind the largest entry in that file, and two thirds sit behind nothing.

**Measured rather than reasoned**, because the line between them was not
obvious: lowering the third case as D2 asks makes `user?.name` a `ref String?`,
and `user?.name ?? "nobody".to_owned()` then has a view on one side of the `??`
and an owned value on the other, with no fit and no way to make one that does
not insert a copy. The receiver is usually a temporary besides, so the view
dangles — which is precisely the refusal the tether analysis would own.

## 2. Decisions

### D1 — A `?.` over a field the compiler knows to **copy** lends its receiver.

`x.as_ref().map(|it| it.a)`, with `and_then` where [ADR-052](adr-052.md) D6
flattens. `user?.id` reads `user` where it lies and `user` is usable on the next
line — [ADR-113](adr-113.md) D1, for the half of D2 that needs no
representation.

**Only where the compiler knows.** The checker records the reaches whose member
copies, by the rule the `keeps` column already uses; a field whose type it could
not work out, and a field that would come out as a view, are left exactly as
they lowered before this record. That is the direction that cannot break a
program that worked ([C.4](../30-nikaia-tooling.md)), and it is why the set is
of the certain cases rather than their complement.

### D2 — A `?.` over a **method** that changes nothing lends its scrutinee.

`match x.as_ref() { Some(it) => …, None => None }`
([ADR-066](adr-066.md) D3's `match`, over a borrowed scrutinee).

**This half needs no representation at all**, and that is the whole reason it
lands here: what comes out of a reached method is the **call's own result**, not
a view of the receiver, so nothing about it reaches the state that is missing.

Asked of the `mutates` column and only where **every** candidate for the name
agrees — `NK1138`'s own rule one construct over. A name this compiler cannot
resolve is claimed nothing about.

### D3 — The third case is unchanged, and it is not refused.

A `?.` over a field that does **not** copy still takes its receiver, and
[ADR-052](adr-052.md) D8's translated note stays for it alone, narrowed to say
so: *`?.` over a member that is not copied takes the value it reaches through*.

**Not refused**, although a refusal is what stands where Tethered would
everywhere else in this compiler (`NK2302`, `NK2303`). The difference is what
exists: those refuse programs that were already refused below. This one would
refuse `find(1)?.name ?? "nobody".to_owned()`, which compiles and runs today —
and refusing a correct program is the one thing this compiler may never do
([C.4](../30-nikaia-tooling.md)).

## 3. Consequences

* **Two of the three shapes a `?.` has stop moving their receiver**, and the
  most common of them — `find(1)?.greet(…)` — is the one the tests were already
  written around.
* **The translated note gets narrower and stays true.** A message that explained
  a move that no longer happens would be worse than the backend's.
* **[#192](https://github.com/Nikaia-Language/Nikaia/issues/192) stops reading as ready.** It was the entry I picked as
  the next small one, and it is two thirds small and one third behind [#196](https://github.com/Nikaia-Language/Nikaia/issues/196).
* **What it costs:** two sets carried from the checker to the emitter, and one
  `as_ref()` in each of two lowerings.

## 4. What this does not decide

* **The view half**, which is D2 of [ADR-113](adr-113.md) and waits on
  [#196](https://github.com/Nikaia-Language/Nikaia/issues/196) with everything else that wants a
  state between Borrowed and Owned.
* **`?.` on the left of an assignment**, which [ADR-113](adr-113.md) §4 already
  leaves out and the language does not have.
* **Whether `??` should join a view to an owned fallback.** It is the question
  the third case runs into first, and it belongs with the state rather than with
  the operator.

## 5. What is built

D1, D2 and D3, at 0.0.140. D4 is a sentence in a notes page.

* `Checked::copied_reaches` and `Checked::lent_reaches`, filled where the
  checker knows and read by the emitter's two `?.` lowerings — the way
  [ADR-028](adr-028.md) hands the emitter every answer it has no types for.
* The translation in `diagnostics`, narrowed to the one case that still moves.
* `crates/nikaia/tests/nullable.rs`: the two shapes **run** with the receiver
  used twice, the unresolved reach is shown to lower as it did, and the third
  case is pinned as a lowering rather than as a refusal — so the day the tether
  lands, the test that has to change says so.
