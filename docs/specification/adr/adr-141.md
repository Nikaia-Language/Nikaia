# ADR-141: Six Things the Specification Writes Are Corrections, and Three Are Constructs That Stay Unspecified

**Status:** Accepted · **Built:** yes — see §5
**Date:** September 19, 2026
**Target Version:** Nikaia 0.0.18
**Related:** [ADR-066](adr-066.md) (`null` and the `T?`), [ADR-025](adr-025.md) D2 (a failure leaves without a sigil), [ADR-023](adr-023.md) D1 (an error type is an `enum`), Part I 4.4, 6.6, 7.1 and 8, `docs/language-review.md` §3.5

## 1. Context

`language-review.md` §3.5 lists nine things the specification writes that the
language does not have. They are not one kind of thing, and telling the two
kinds apart is the whole of this record.

**Six are slips**: a page reaching for a surface the language deliberately does
not have, or writing a type by a name it does not carry. Each has a correct
spelling that exists today, so each is a page edit with no ruling behind it.

**Three are constructs**: a duration literal, a channel and a `select` block.
Each is a feature the language would gain, each belongs to Part I chapter 8,
and none of them has a record. A page that writes one is not a slip — it is a
page ahead of a decision nobody has taken.

## 2. Decisions

### D1 — The six are corrected on the page, and each correction is the spelling that exists.

| written | is |
| :--- | :--- |
| `neg.is_some()` (`examples/1brc.nika`) | `neg != null` — there is no `Some` here ([ADR-066](adr-066.md)) |
| `io::read_to_string("input.txt")?` (Part III C.4) | `io::read_to_string("input.txt")` — a failure leaves on its own ([ADR-025](adr-025.md) D2) |
| `List[Token]`, `List[Row]`, `List[Fortune]` | `Vec[…]` — Part I 2.2's prose calls the concept *List (Vector)* and the type is `Vec` |
| `panic::on_panic fn(info) sync { … }` | the hook without the word: a lambda carries no promise ([ADR-102](adr-102.md) D1 puts it on the *parameter's type*) |
| `supervisor::start_link(…; restart_policy: "always")` | an `enum`, which is what Part I 4.4 argues for where a string names one of a few things |
| `TimeoutError("Too slow!")` | an `enum` variant, because an error type is an `enum` ([ADR-023](adr-023.md) D1) |

The first three are wrong about a **spelling**; the last three are wrong about
a **shape**, and each of those three is a page writing the thing the page next
to it argues against.

### D2 — The three constructs are marked *unspecified*, in place.

`5.seconds()`, `channel::bounded(100)` and `select { … }` stay on their pages
and each gains one line: **unspecified — a construct with no record, decided
when Part I 8 is next opened**.

**Marked rather than removed**, and rather than decided here. Removing them
loses the shape somebody wrote down and the argument it was making; deciding
them now would be three language features taken in a footnote, when each is a
record's worth of question — what a duration *is* as a type, whether a channel
is `std`'s or the language's, and what `select` does to `sync`.

### D3 — The mark is a form, and the specification gets one.

> **Unspecified.** This writes a construct the language does not have and no
> record decides. It is here for the shape of the example; the decision is
> named where it will be taken.

One paragraph, beside the **Status** note's own definition, so that a reader
who meets the mark knows what it promises: nothing, yet, and where to look.

## 3. Consequences

* Six examples stop teaching a surface the language does not have — and the
  three that are wrong about a shape stop contradicting the page beside them.
* A reader who meets `select { … }` is told it is unspecified rather than
  left to find out by compiling it.
* **What it costs:** six edits, three marks, and one paragraph of convention.

## 4. What this does not decide

* **The duration literal**, **the channel** and **`select`**. Each is Part I
  chapter 8's, and this record's whole point about them is that they are not
  decided here.
* **Whether the specification's other unbuilt examples get the mark.** They
  are ahead of the *compiler* and their records exist, which is a different
  state and has the **Status** note it already has.

## 5. What is built

**All of it**, because none of it is compiler work: six page edits, one line of
`examples/1brc.nika`, one paragraph of convention and three marks.

**The `1brc.nika` line was the only one that could break a program**, and it is
the only one that was checked by running the compiler rather than by reading.
`neg.is_some()` is inside a grammar action, where `neg` is the `T?` that
`"-"?` produces; it lowered to a method call on a receiver nothing typed, which
is the silence [ADR-105](adr-105.md) §1 counted. `neg != null` lowers to
`neg != None` and the file lowers as it did.

**D3's paragraph is in `docs/specification/README.md` and not on Part III**,
which is where this record said to put it and was wrong about: the **Status**
note's own definition lives there, in the conventions that govern all three
parts, and a second convention for the same reader belongs beside the first one
rather than inside the part that happens to hold the appendices. The paragraph
says what the two marks are *not* — one stands under a decided rule the
compiler has not caught up with, the other under a construct nobody decided —
because that distinction is the whole of D2.

**The `select` block carries both marks**, and that is not a mistake: the
construct is unspecified (D2) *and* the page states a teardown rule for it that
[ADR-006](adr-006.md) D3 decided, which is a **Status** note's job. They say
different things and stand one under the other.

*And all three marks are gone one round later*, which is the shortest life a
convention has had here: [ADR-148](adr-148.md) decides `select`,
[ADR-149](adr-149.md) the channel and [ADR-150](adr-150.md) the duration, so
each of the three now carries a **Status** note instead. D3's paragraph stays
on [`specification/README.md`](../README.md) — the form is what this record
added, and the next construct written ahead of a decision will want it.

**Two corrections reached further than the table says.** `List[…]` is written
in **four** places rather than three — Part I 7.1, Part II 11's `execute`,
Part III 17.1's `Bytes` sentence and Appendix B's `map` signature — and all
four say `Vec` now. The `Fortune` one the table names is not on a page of the
specification at all.

*Nothing is left.* The three constructs are only what Part I chapter 8 will
decide, which is scope and not work.
