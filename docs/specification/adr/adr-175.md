## 1. Context

The build-time evaluator could loop and could call, and had nowhere to put what
a loop produced. [ADR-079](adr-079.md) §3 named the gap and did not shorten it —
*"an evaluator that can loop and push … this is the real work behind the
feature"* — and [#174](https://github.com/Nikaia-Language/Nikaia/issues/174) carried it as *no
`push` and no aggregate value*, with two records waiting behind it.

What a `comptime` handed down was what a Rust `const` can hold, and that was one
integer or one `bool`. So [ADR-079](adr-079.md) §3's table could be **computed**
and had nowhere to arrive.

## 2. Decisions

### D1 — The aggregate is an array, and it is what a `push` crosses as.

> **Corrected at 0.0.111, and the heading is the correction.** This decision was
> written *and there is no `push`*, with the measurement below as the reason.
> The measurement is right and the conclusion drawn from it was too narrow: what
> `NK1104` refuses is a **function's declared result**, and a `comptime` is not
> one. By the time a declaration is compared the build has computed the value,
> so its length is a fact — which is [ADR-079](adr-079.md)'s own title,
> *growable going in, fixed coming out*, and that record is now built for a
> list. What stands unchanged is everything after the first sentence: the
> crossed form is `[T; N]`, and a `Vec` still cannot be what a `const` holds.

`Value::List` in the evaluator, landing as a Rust `const` array:
`comptime TABLE: Array[i64, 5] = squares()` becomes
`const TABLE: [i64; 5] = [0, 1, 4, 9, 16];`.

**Measured rather than chosen.** `.push` on a list hands back a `Vec[?]`, and a
`Vec` allocates, so a `const` cannot hold one; `NK1104` refuses a `Vec[?]`
against an `Array[i64, 5]` long before this evaluator is reached. `[T; N]` is
exactly what a `const` holds ([ADR-152](adr-152.md)). So a build-time table is
**written at its length and filled by index**, and [#174](https://github.com/Nikaia-Language/Nikaia/issues/174)'s *push* is answered by
the shape the type system already had rather than by a new one.

What the evaluator gained for it: the list literal, `xs[i]`, `xs[i] = …`,
`xs[i] += …` and `xs.len()` — the four things writing a table by hand needs and
nothing else.

### D2 — An index the array does not have is refused by name (`NK1165`).

`xs[7]` of three elements, in a body being run while the program is built.
[ADR-285](adr-285.md) D1 aborts with this sentence at run time; here there is no
run to abort in, and answering *this compiler cannot evaluate it* would send the
reader looking for a missing feature instead of at the line
([Part III C.2](../30-nikaia-tooling.md)).

**And a refusal by name is said once.** `NK1152` and `NK1165` each say what
`NK1127` would, with the part that matters in it — so `NK1127` is kept for the
case it is actually about, a shape this evaluator does not read. Two errors for
one mistake is what the old shape printed.

### D3 — An array knows its own length in the ledger.

`Array::len` and `Array::is_empty` in `std.contracts`. `Array[T, N]` carries its
length in its type and lowers to `[T; N]`, which has both — and nothing said so,
so `xs.len()` on one resolved to no entry. It lowered and it ran; what it cost
was the **enclosing** function's touch set, because an unresolved call is
unknown ([ADR-292](adr-292.md)). The visible price was that
`for i in 0..<xs.len()` — the natural spelling — made a function uncallable from
a `comptime` with *nothing says what it touches*, while `0..<5` was fine.

## 3. Consequences

**The compiler stopped crashing on a correct program.** `expect` is called with
the word `"const"` for a `comptime` whose value disagrees with its type, and
that word had no diagnostic code: an `unreachable!`, reached by
`comptime PRIMES: Array[i64, 4] = [2, 3, 5, 7]`. That is
[Part I 6.8](../10-nikaia-light.md)'s *a raw internal error reaching you is a
Nikaia bug*, met by the compiler on itself. The word has `NK1166` now, and the
literal it crashed on is answered by its annotation the way a `let`'s is
([ADR-152](adr-152.md) D4) — one line that was in the `let` path and not here.

**[ADR-079](adr-079.md) §3's table has somewhere to arrive**, which is what the
three records waiting on §2.8 were waiting for.

## 4. What this does not decide

* ~~**A `Vec` at build time.** D1 answers why there is none, and nothing wants
  one: what would carry it down does not exist.~~ **Asked and answered at
  0.0.111**: a `Vec` in the *body* is [ADR-079](adr-079.md) D1's growable half,
  and what carries it down is the array this record built. A `Vec` as the
  **crossed** form stays impossible, which is what the sentence was right
  about — `NK1167` says so by name now.
* **Text.** A `String` at build time is its own question and no record asks it.
* **An array of arrays.** The evaluator holds one, and the landing says nothing
  rather than guessing a type with a length per level.
* **The walk over a type's fields** ([ADR-088](adr-088.md) D1, Part II 10.3),
  which is [#174](https://github.com/Nikaia-Language/Nikaia/issues/174)'s *after it* and waits on
  [#178](https://github.com/Nikaia-Language/Nikaia/issues/178) — running a grammar while the
  program is built is not interpretation.

## 5. What is built

All of D1, D2 and D3.

* `build_time::Value::List`, the list literal, `Expr::Index`, an indexed
  assignment (with and without an operator) and `xs.len()`.
* `rust_constant_type` reads `Array[T, N]` as `[T; N]`; a table with no declared
  type takes its element type from its elements.
* `NK1165`, `NK1166`, and `NK1127` withheld where a refusal was already made by
  name.
* `Array::len` and `Array::is_empty` in `std.contracts`, which the ledger's own
  length guard in `tests/contracts.rs` had to be told about — that test is what
  makes adding a fifth `::len` a decision rather than a line.
* `crates/nikaia/tests/build_time.rs`: the computed table, the hand-written one,
  the two refusals, the one-error-per-mistake rule at both codes, and the ledger
  entry.
