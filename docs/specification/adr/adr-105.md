# ADR-105: The Ledger Says `Seq[T]` for What Is Produced Step by Step, and `Par[T]` Where the Steps Run at Once

**Status:** Accepted · **Built:** steps 1, 2 and 3 — see §5
**Date:** September 15, 2026
**Target Version:** Nikaia 0.0.8
**Related:** [ADR-031](adr-031.md) (the ledger's type language, whose words
these join), [ADR-024](adr-024.md) (`?` is the absence of a claim),
[ADR-102](adr-102.md) D1 and D2 (`sync` and `throws` after a type, which a
step borrows), [ADR-094](adr-094.md) D2 and D4 (a value walked is a value
kept), [ADR-025](adr-025.md) D1 (a loop's step can fail, and the function
declares it), Part II 12.6 (`par_iter` and the `sync` lambda)

## 1. Context

The ledger's type language has a name, type arguments, a variable, a view,
a tuple, a function type and `?`. It has no word for what `keys()`,
`chars()`, `io::lines()` and `xs.map fn …` hand back: elements of a type,
produced one at a time when asked for, with no length until the end and
nothing laid out in memory. So those entries say `-> ?`, and because a
method is found through its receiver's type, everything called on such a
value is unfound too — nine `std` entries with a `?` result, and every one
of the 35 unanswered method calls in the corpus downstream of one.

A container is not this. A `Vec[T]` has its elements already, a length, and
an index; it is walked as often as one likes. A produced sequence is walked
once, and each step may do work: a step of `io::lines()` reads the pipe, so
it may pause and may fail, which Part I 3.3 already turns into a `throws` on
the function. What is missing is the type the property hangs on.

## 2. Decisions

### D1 — `Seq[T]` is a word of the ledger's type language, and what a step may do stands after it as it stands after a function type.

`Seq[T]` is elements of `T`, produced step by step. `sync` and `throws`
after it say what one step may do, with [ADR-102](adr-102.md) D2's reading:
without `sync` a step may pause, without `throws` it cannot fail.

```toml
[fn."HashMap::keys"]
signature = "(&HashMap[$K, $V]) -> Seq[$K] sync"

[fn."io::lines"]
signature = "() -> Seq[String] throws"

[fn."Seq::collect"]
signature = "(Seq[$T]) -> Vec[$T]"
```

A container keeps its own name. `Vec[T]`, `HashMap[K, V]` and a range are
walked *as* a `Seq` by a `for`, and are not one.

### D2 — A `Seq` is consumed by walking it, and a second walk is refused.

`for`, `collect`, `count`, `join`, `nth` and every other method that walks
take the `Seq` by value: it is a value kept ([ADR-094](adr-094.md) D2), and a
use after it is refused in this compiler's words — *this sequence was
already walked*. That is the whole of the once-only rule; no word carries
it. A container is walked by view ([ADR-094](adr-094.md) D4) and as often as
one likes.

### D3 — `Par[T]` is what `par_iter()` hands back, and a lambda handed to it must be `sync`.

The one dimension that needs a second word, because a caller's rule
differs: a lambda given to a method on a `Par[T]` runs on several cores at
once and must be `sync` (Part II 12.6), where one given to a `Seq[T]` may
pause. Otherwise `Par[T]` has `Seq[T]`'s surface, and at
`user_parallelism = no` it walks in order on one thread, which Part II 10.7
already promises of a `par_fold`.

### D4 — Neither word is in the surface type grammar.

A program cannot write `Seq[T]` or `Par[T]`; it writes `keys()`, `for`,
`collect()`, and the ledger does the naming. A function of the program's
own that hands back a produced sequence is a decision about laziness at the
surface, and it is taken the day a program asks for it
([ADR-028](adr-028.md) D5), not here.

### D5 — What is not distinguished, on purpose.

Whether the elements are views into a buffer is the element type's
(`Seq[&str]`, with Part I 6.6's rules); whether the order is stable is the
container's (`HashMap` has none); whether the length is known is D1's split.
No word for any of the three.

## 3. Consequences

The chain `keys().collect()` is `Seq[$K]` then `Vec[$K]`, `names.sort()`
finds its method, and the 35 stop being silent. `throws` on a `Seq` is the
one spelling of *a step can fail*, which is what a loop over `io::lines()`
already costs the function.

**What a reader gains** is a signature that says what `map` does to what it
is given — `(Seq[$T], f: fn($T) -> $U) -> Seq[$U]` — where it said `?`.

**What it costs:** the entries of every `std` function that produces or
consumes a sequence, rewritten; a receiver rule in the checker for the two
words; and the once-only refusal, which is `keeps` asked of a `Seq`.

## 4. What this does not decide

* **The surface word.** D4; it is a laziness decision and waits for a
  program.
* **A `Seq` that is also indexable**, a range with a length. It is a
  container here and walked as a `Seq`; a program that wants both at once
  on one value has not been written.

## 5. What is built

**Steps 1 and 2.** `Seq[T]` and `Par[T]` are words of the ledger's type language:
they parse with the two trailing words, write themselves back, bind through their
item, answer as a receiver (`Seq::collect` is found exactly as `Vec::push` is,
and a `Par` falls back to `Seq`'s entries for D3's *otherwise `Par[T]` has
`Seq[T]`'s surface*), and a `for` over one binds its item. A program cannot write
either (D4), and `NK1135` is what says so — by the rule that refuses `Widgit` and
not by a special case.

The entries: `HashMap::keys`, `HashMap::values`, `String::chars`, `Vec::drain`,
`HashMap::drain`, `io::lines`, and six consumers under the receiver's own word —
`Seq::collect`, `Seq::count`, `Seq::nth`, `Seq::join`, `Seq::map`, `Seq::filter`.

**`io::lines` was the one that mattered, and the record's example is why.** It
said `-> Lines`, a named type whose `iterates = "throws"` column carried the
failing step — which worked for the `for` and for nothing else: the binding had no
type, so `line.len()` inside the loop was a method on `?`, and six of
`examples/tally/src/main.nika`'s `.len()` calls were among the 35 §1 counts.
`Seq[String] throws` says both things in one place, and *`throws` on a `Seq` is
the one spelling of a step can fail* (§3) is now literally true — the fallible-step
rule reads either.

**The message describes a `Seq` rather than naming it.** D4 says a program cannot
write `Seq[String]`, so `NK2701` reads *a `for` over a sequence of `String` binds
one name*: a message that names a spelling its reader has no way to type is the
shape [Part III C.1](../30-nikaia-tooling.md) is about. It said `Lines` before,
which the program could not write either.

**And one claim of §1 did not survive the measurement.** *Every one of the 35
unanswered method calls in the corpus downstream of a `?` that is a sequence* is
not what the corpus shows. The harness now in
`crates/nikaia/tests/sequences.rs` reads **38** before these entries and **33**
after, and the trace behind it says the rest are downstream of a receiver with no
type for other reasons — `tail.drain()` in `json.nika` where `tail` is a field
nothing types, `map` on the result of a `catch` in `access-log.nika`. Those are
somebody else's entry, and `MethodCalls::unanswered` exists so the number can be
checked again rather than remembered.

**`par_iter` has no entry**, and that is D4's *it waits for a program* read
strictly: nothing in `examples/`, in `tests/` or in `std` calls it. `par_fold` is
a grammar driver and not this. The **word** is in the type language and binds, so
the entry is one line the day something asks for it.

**Step 3, D2's once-only rule, is `NK2702`.** A name whose type is a `Seq` and
that a walk consumed is refused where it is read again: *`lines` is a sequence
that was already walked*. What counts as a walk is read **off the signature** and
not off a list of names — every `Seq` entry writes its receiver `(Seq[$T], …)` and
a container's writes `(&Vec[$T], …)`, so the file that describes the method is
what says whether the walk keeps it — and a `for` over one is the other half.

*It is `NK2101`'s analysis with one word changed*, which is why it sits beside it
and is answered at the same moment: the shape is the same question — a name used
after something took it — an assignment in between **revives** it, and only a
plain name is asked about, because `map.keys().collect()` walks a temporary and a
temporary has no second use to refuse. It has a tally line of its own for the
reason the two `NK21xx` rules do: the way out is a `collect()` and not a `throws`,
and *a place that can fail without saying so* is not what it counted.

*And the fit had no arm for the new type*, which said that a `Seq[String] throws`
did not fit a `Seq[String] throws`. Found by the probe that gives a name another
sequence, whose message read *this is X, and what it is assigned to is X*. Two
sequences fit when their items do, with the two words read as a function type's
are — and `Par` fits `Seq` and not the other way round, which is D3: a `Par`'s
surface *is* a `Seq`'s, and a `Seq` is not promised to run at once.

**What is left is step 4:** D3's `sync` demand on a
`Par[T]`'s lambda, which waits on `par_iter` having an entry to demand it of.
`docs/open-work.md` carries it.

*Fourteen tests* in `crates/nikaia/tests/sequences.rs`.
